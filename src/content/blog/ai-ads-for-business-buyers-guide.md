If you've searched for "AI ads" recently, you'll have noticed the results are a mix of very different things: ad generator apps promising an advert in seconds, freelancers offering AI videos, and agencies selling AI advertising as a service. They all use the same two letters, but they're not the same product, and choosing the wrong one is how businesses end up with adverts that look generic, misrepresent their product or never get used.

This buyer's guide explains your options in plain terms, helps you work out which fits your business, and gives you the questions to ask before you spend anything.

> **Key takeaways**
>
> - **"AI ads" means three different things:** do-it-yourself ad generators, freelancers using AI tools, and AI ads agencies.
> - **DIY tools are cheapest** but cost you time, and struggle to keep real products accurate.
> - **Agencies cost more per advert** but deliver finished, platform-ready adverts, usually in days.
> - **The right choice depends** on how many adverts you need, how much time you have and how exact your product must look.
> - **Always see a sample made from your own product** before you pay.

## What "AI Ads" Actually Means

When businesses search for AI ads, they're usually looking for one of three things:

1. **AI-made adverts.** Video and image adverts produced with AI rather than filmed with a camera crew. This is what most people mean, and it's what this guide focuses on.
2. **AI ad generators.** Apps and websites where you create adverts yourself from templates and prompts.
3. **AI-powered ad platforms.** The automated targeting and bidding built into Meta, Google and TikTok. Every advertiser already uses these whether they know it or not, so they're not really something you buy separately.

The real decision is how you get the first one: by making AI ads yourself, hiring a freelancer or working with an AI ads agency.

## Your Three Options Compared

| | DIY AI ad generator | Freelancer using AI | AI ads agency |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Typical cost** | Free tiers to monthly subscriptions | Varies widely by person | Packages, e.g. from R3,000 at Aiadverts |
| **Your time** | High: you make every advert | Medium: briefing and feedback | Low: a short brief and a review |
| **Product accuracy** | Hard to control | Depends on skill | Should be checked as standard |
| **Platform formats** | You resize everything | Sometimes included | Usually included |
| **Consistency over time** | Up to you | Depends on availability | Built into the service |
| **Best for** | Experiments, tight budgets, hands-on owners | One-off projects | Regular adverts that represent the brand |

None of these is wrong. A hands-on owner with time and a simple product can do well with a generator. A business that needs adverts every month, and needs its products to look exactly right, usually gets far better value from an agency.

## What a Good AI Ad Should Include

Whichever route you choose, judge every AI ad against the same checklist:

- **A hook in the first second or two.** People decide almost instantly whether to keep watching.
- **Your real product, shown accurately.** Wrong packaging, garbled labels or the wrong colour will cost you trust, and can mislead customers.
- **One clear offer.** A single message beats a list of everything you sell.
- **The right shape for the platform.** Vertical 9:16 for Reels, TikTok and WhatsApp Status, 4:5 for feeds, 16:9 for YouTube. Our [video ad sizes guide](/blog/social-media-video-ad-sizes) covers every format and safe zone.
- **On-screen text or captions,** because many people scroll with the sound off.
- **A clear next step,** whether that's "Order now", "Book today" or "Message us on WhatsApp".

## Questions to Ask Before You Buy AI Ads

Before you pay any provider, whether it's a freelancer or an AI ads agency, get clear answers to these:

1. **Can I see examples from businesses like mine?** A portfolio of real client work tells you more than any sales pitch.
2. **Will you use my actual products?** Ask how they keep packaging, labels and colours accurate.
3. **Can I see a sample first?** A free demo made from your own product is the fairest test of quality.
4. **How many revisions are included?** And what happens if the first draft misses the mark?
5. **Which formats will I receive?** You want every size your platforms need, not one file you have to resize.
6. **How long does delivery take?** Days or weeks makes a real difference to promotions and launches.
7. **How am I allowed to use the adverts?** Confirm you can run them on every platform and for as long as you need.
8. **Is there a contract, and how long is it?** Make sure you can start small and scale up.
9. **Do you handle posting and reporting,** or just the adverts themselves?

A good provider will answer all of these without hesitation. Our guide on [how to brief an AI advert](/blog/how-to-brief-an-ai-advert-south-africa) also shows what a well-run process asks from you in return.

## Red Flags to Watch For

- **No real portfolio,** or only generic stock-looking examples that could be anyone's.
- **Reluctance to show your actual product.** If they can't make your product look right in a sample, they won't in a campaign.
- **Promises of guaranteed sales or "going viral".** Nobody can honestly guarantee results from creative alone.
- **Long lock-in contracts** before you've seen a single advert.
- **Vague answers about how you can use the adverts.**
- **Claims that look too good to be true** in the adverts themselves. In South Africa, adverts must still be truthful under the Advertising Regulatory Board's Code, whether AI made them or not, as our [complete guide to AI advertising](/blog/ai-advertising-south-africa-guide) explains.
