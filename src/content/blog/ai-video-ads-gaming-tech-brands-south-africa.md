Gaming peripherals and consumer tech are sold almost entirely on the strength of the visual. A headset, a controller, a mechanical keyboard — the purchase decision happens in a few seconds of video, not a product description. The brands that dominate this space on social media — the ones getting the views, the shares, the impulse saves — are producing cinematic product films that make hardware look like it belongs in a blockbuster trailer.

For an international brand with a marketing department, that's standard. For a South African gaming brand competing in the same feed, matching that production standard has historically been out of reach.

## What Gaming Product Video Actually Requires

The visual language of gaming marketing is specific: dramatic lighting, motion, energy. The kind of video that works for a headset isn't a white-background product shot — it's close-up detail on the mesh, the RGB cycle in slow motion, the ear cushion compression in macro. Getting that right traditionally means:

- A product videographer with experience in consumer electronics
- Controlled studio lighting capable of isolating specific product details
- Motion tracking or gimbal equipment for dynamic product movement
- Post-production to add lighting effects, colour grading and motion graphics
- Multiple rounds of cut-down edits for different platforms and aspect ratios

A production day capable of delivering content at that standard easily runs R20,000–R40,000 before editing. For a brand running multiple product lines or seasonal drops, that becomes a structural barrier — not a one-off cost.
