South Africa produces world-class wine, beer, gin and cider, and most of the smaller producers behind it market themselves with the same two or three photographs all year round. A bottle on a white background. A vineyard at harvest. Maybe a glass on a table, shot by whoever had the best phone that day.

It isn't a lack of ambition. Drinks branding lives or dies on atmosphere, and atmosphere is expensive to photograph. The same bottle needs to look crisp and cold in a summer campaign, warm and candlelit in winter, elegant in a gifting campaign and rugged and outdoorsy for a braai weekend. Traditionally that's four separate shoots, four sets of props, and four days a small producer can't spare during a season already crowded with harvest, production and distribution.

## What Drinks Photography Usually Costs

The visual work behind a drinks campaign is deceptively involved:

- **Styling and props** for each setting: glassware, ice, linen, a table, a location that reads as the right season
- **Lighting a bottle**, which is glass, and therefore reflects everything around it, including the photographer
- **Condensation, pour and splash shots**, which are a specialist skill and rarely work on the first take
- **Location and permissions** for anything shot beyond the cellar door
- **Repeat shoots** for every new vintage, seasonal release, limited run or collaboration

A producer with a range of six labels can easily spend more photographing the range than on the packaging itself.

## A Note on Advertising Alcohol Responsibly

Drinks marketing carries obligations most categories don't. Alcohol advertising in South Africa is governed by industry codes covering who may be targeted, how drinking is portrayed and what claims may be made, and the major platforms apply their own age-restriction settings on top of that.

That is worth raising at briefing stage rather than after a campaign is built. Tell us the rules you work under, and any wording your distributor or importer requires, and the content can be built to fit them. Our guide on [how to brief an AI advert](/blog/how-to-brief-an-ai-advert-south-africa) covers where those non-negotiables belong in a brief. For the legal detail specific to your licence and market, check with your own compliance advisor.
