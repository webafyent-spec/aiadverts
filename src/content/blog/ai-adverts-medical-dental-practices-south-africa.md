Doctors, dentists, physios and clinics are some of the best candidates for video advertising in South Africa, and some of the most cautious. That caution is justified. Healthcare practitioners are regulated by the Health Professions Council of South Africa (HPCSA) and their professional bodies, and a careless advert can lead to a complaint rather than a new patient.

AI adverts can still work well here, as long as you use them for what they do best (explaining, introducing and reminding) and keep them away from what they can't do honestly (proving results). This guide covers where that line sits, with a checker you can use on your own advert copy.

> **Key takeaways**
>
> - **You can advertise.** HPCSA guidance allows it, as long as the advert is not unprofessional, untruthful, deceptive or misleading, and doesn't create unwarranted anxiety.
> - **AI is a good fit for education and branding:** explainers, practice introductions, hours and booking reminders.
> - **AI is a poor fit for proof.** Don't generate patients, results or staff and present them as real.
> - **Wording matters as much as visuals.** "Best", "painless" and "guaranteed" are the words most likely to cause problems.
> - **This is guidance, not legal advice.** Always check final copy with the HPCSA, your professional body or your indemnity provider.

## What the Rules Actually Say

The details sit in the HPCSA's [Ethical and Professional Rules](https://www.hpcsa-blogs.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/Ethics_Booklet-2.pdf) and its [guidelines on social media](https://www.hpcsa-blogs.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Booklet-16-Guidelines-on-Social-Media_March-2025-FINAL.pdf), which were updated in March 2025. In plain terms, the themes are:

- **Advertising is allowed, with limits.** A practitioner may advertise, provided the advert is not unprofessional, untruthful, deceptive or misleading, and does not cause consumers unwarranted anxiety about a health condition.
- **No canvassing or touting.** That includes having someone else do it on your behalf, and, as a [summary of the rules](https://genoa.co.za/2026/07/31/the-ethical-rules-of-conduct-what-healthcare-practitioners-must-know/) points out, disguised influencer endorsement or commission tied to patient conversion.
- **Claims must be supportable.** Statements such as "guaranteed cure", "risk-free treatment" or "the best specialist in South Africa" are hard to justify.
- **Patient content needs written consent.** HPCSA guidance requires written consent before publishing case histories or photographs, whether or not you think the patient can be identified.
- **Your details must be accurate.** Names, titles and qualifications should match your registration.

South Africa's general advertising rules also apply. The [Advertising Regulatory Board's Code](https://www.arb.org.za/) covers truthfulness in all adverts, and our [complete guide to AI advertising](/blog/ai-advertising-south-africa-guide) covers how it applies to AI-produced content.

None of this is a reason to avoid advertising. It's a reason to be deliberate about what each advert is for.

## Where AI Adverts Work for Practices

The best medical and dental adverts don't claim anything. They help a patient feel comfortable and know what to do next.

- **Patient-education explainers.** A short animated advert on what happens at a first check-up, how long a scan takes, or when children should first see a dentist. These remove the fear of the unknown, which is the biggest barrier to booking.
- **Practice introductions.** A brand film that shows your values, location and team, built from your real branding and genuine photos.
- **Practical information.** New location, new opening hours, new services, new doctor joining the practice, medical aid information.
- **Reminders and seasonal awareness.** Check-ups before school, flu season reminders, a new-year dental check, scheduled around the dates in our [South African marketing calendar](/blog/south-african-marketing-calendar-2026-2027).
- **WhatsApp Status.** A short, clear advert with booking details in the format patients already use. See our [WhatsApp Status guide](/blog/whatsapp-status-video-ads-south-africa).
- **Recruitment.** Hiring a receptionist, hygienist or locum.

Because AI adverts are made without a film crew, there are no patients to book, rooms to light or staff to take out of the schedule, which is often the real obstacle for a busy practice.

## Which Advert Ideas Are Safe?

Treat this as a quick guide to risk, not a ruling. The right answer for any specific advert depends on your profession and current rules.

| Advert idea | Risk | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Animated explainer of a common procedure or check-up | Lower | Keep it factual and calm, and avoid outcomes or comparisons. |
| Practice introduction, hours, location, new services | Lower | Make sure names, titles and qualifications match your registration. |
| Seasonal check-up or awareness reminder | Lower | Keep the tone informative rather than frightening. |
| Staff profile with qualifications | Check | Qualifications and titles must be accurate and match your registration. |
| Price or special offer | Check | Be accurate and clear, and avoid pressure wording. Confirm the rules on fees. |
| Health statistics | Check | Cite a real source and avoid wording that creates anxiety. |
| Patient testimonial | Higher | Needs written consent, confidentiality and representativeness. Many practices avoid it. |
| Real before-and-after photos | Higher | Needs written consent (parent or guardian for minors) and must be unedited. |
| AI-generated "patients", results or reviews presented as real | Avoid | This is misleading by design. |
| AI-enhanced before-and-after images | Avoid | Altering results deceives the public. |
| AI-generated "doctor" shown as your real staff member | Avoid | Presenting someone who doesn't exist as a practitioner is deceptive. |
| Guarantees, "painless", "best in the country" | Avoid | Hard to support and likely to be found misleading. |

The rule of thumb is simple: **use AI to explain and introduce, and never to prove.** If an AI-generated image could lead a patient to believe it shows a real person, real result or real review, don't use it.

## Seven Rules for Safer AI Adverts

1. **Explain, don't promise.** Describe the service and what the patient can expect. Avoid outcomes.
2. **Never generate proof.** No synthetic patients, results, reviews or "case studies" shown as real.
3. **Be honest about who is on screen.** If a person appears, they should be a real member of your team (with their consent), or clearly an illustrated character, not an AI "doctor" who looks like a real practitioner.
4. **Get written consent for any patient content.** And remember that consent doesn't make a misleading image acceptable.
5. **Keep your titles accurate.** Match them to your registration exactly.
6. **Keep the tone calm.** Inform, don't frighten.
7. **Have it checked.** Send final copy to the HPCSA, your professional association or your indemnity provider before it goes live.

POPIA also matters here. Because health information is [special personal information](https://inforegulator.org.za/), anything that could identify a patient, including a photo, a first name next to a procedure or a screenshot of a booking, needs proper consent and handling.
