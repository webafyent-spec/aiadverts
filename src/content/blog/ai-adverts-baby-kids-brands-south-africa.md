Few buyers research as carefully as new parents. Before they add a bottle, a pram, a baby cream or a box of teething toys to the cart, they're checking reviews, comparing brands and looking closely at every photo for signs of quality and care. For a baby or kids brand, the product imagery is doing much of the selling, and a quick phone photo on the kitchen counter rarely earns that trust.

Yet that's how many small South African baby and kids brands still market themselves, because the alternative is expensive and complicated. Proper lifestyle photography means a stylist, a set, a photographer and, very often, children on camera, with all the consent, privacy and scheduling that involves.

## Why Baby and Kids Marketing Is Different

A few things make this category harder to photograph and advertise than most:

- **Trust comes first.** Parents look for cues of safety, cleanliness and quality in every image before they buy.
- **Children on camera are complicated.** You need parental consent, careful handling of how the images are used, and a shoot that works around naps and moods.
- **The range keeps growing.** New sizes, colours, age stages and gift sets each need their own imagery.
- **Gifting drives big moments.** Baby showers, birthdays and the festive season all need seasonal content on time.
- **Every claim is scrutinised.** Words like "gentle", "natural" or "safe" carry more weight here than in almost any other category.

## Advertising Responsibly to Parents

Baby and kids brands carry extra responsibility, and it's worth building that into your adverts from the start:

- **Speak to parents, not children.** The Advertising Regulatory Board's Code of Advertising Practice has specific provisions for advertising aimed at or featuring children, so adverts for young children's products should be pitched at the grown-ups who buy them.
- **Back up every claim.** "Hypoallergenic", "BPA-free", "dermatologically tested" and similar claims must be true and supportable. An AI advert should never make a product look safer or more capable than it is.
- **Show the product accurately.** Colours, sizes and features in the advert must match what arrives at the door.
