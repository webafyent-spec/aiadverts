Your product photo does more selling than your product description, your reviews or your price. On a marketplace or an online store, it's the only part of the product a customer can see, and they decide in seconds.

That's why so many South African sellers are looking at AI product photography. Traditional studio work is slow and priced per shoot, per image or per SKU, which gets expensive fast for a growing catalogue. AI can produce lifestyle scenes, colour variations and seasonal versions in days instead of weeks.

But there's a catch that most guides skip: an AI image can look completely real and still show a product that isn't the one you sell. This guide explains what AI product photography actually is, where it's safe to use, where you still need a real photo, what Takealot and Amazon expect, and how to check an image before it goes live.

> **Key takeaways**
>
> - **AI product photography** ranges from cleaning up a real photo to generating a whole scene. The more of the product that's generated, the more carefully it must be checked.
> - **Realism is not fidelity.** An image can look like a photograph and still misrepresent your product. Fidelity is what protects you from returns and bad reviews.
> - **Keep the shots that must be exact based on real photos:** the main marketplace image, labels, colour-critical details and true scale.
> - **Use AI where a set would otherwise be needed:** lifestyle scenes, variations, seasonal versions and advert creative.
> - **Marketplaces are strict.** Takealot and Amazon both want a plain white main image with the product filling most of the frame.

## What Is AI Product Photography?

AI product photography is the use of artificial intelligence to create, edit or extend images of physical products. It replaces some of what a studio, a set and a stylist do: the lighting, the background, the surface, the setting.

It isn't one thing. It helps to think of three levels:

| Level | What happens | Risk to accuracy | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AI-enhanced** | A real photo is cleaned up: background removed, lighting and shadows improved | Low | Marketplace listings, product pages |
| **AI-composed** | Your real product is placed into an AI-built scene | Medium | Lifestyle images, adverts, social posts |
| **AI-generated** | The product itself is generated from reference photos | Higher | Concept and advert creative, never evidence of the item |

Most confusion, and most bad results, come from treating these as the same thing. A sensible product photography plan uses all three, but for different jobs.

## Realism Is Not Fidelity

Here is the single most useful idea in this guide. When you look at an AI product image, there are two separate questions:

- **Realism:** does this look like a real photograph?
- **Fidelity:** is this exactly the item the customer will receive?

Modern AI passes the first question easily. The second is where products get misrepresented. A bottle can look perfectly photographed while its label text is subtly garbled, its colour has drifted, or it has quietly lost a component.

This isn't a theoretical worry. In [Photoroom's 2026 survey](https://www.photoroom.com/blog/fidelity-gap-ai-product-photography) of 322 enterprise leaders, 37% named inaccurate or misrepresented visuals as their top concern with AI image production. Its analysis of AI-generated product images found the most common errors were:

- **Logo and text distortion:** the most frequent fault, in about 20% of generations tested
- **Missing elements:** about 12.5%
- **Pattern or design changes:** about 11.4%
- **Colour shifts:** about 8.1%

Notice what tops the list: text and logos. If your product has a label, that's exactly the part to check most carefully.

The cost of getting this wrong is practical. Customers who receive something that doesn't match the photo return it, leave a review, or both. And in South Africa there's a legal side too: Section 41 of the [Consumer Protection Act](https://www.polity.org.za/article/the-consumer-protection-act-and-its-impact-on-advertising-and-marketing-in-south-africa-2025-02-28) prohibits false, misleading or deceptive representations about a material fact, and the Advertising Regulatory Board's Code requires advertising to be truthful. Neither cares whether a human or an AI made the image.

### A 60-second check before you publish

Put the AI image next to the real product, or your original photo, and check:

1. **Colour.** Does it match the physical item, in daylight?
2. **Label and text.** Is every word spelled correctly and legible, with the right logo?
3. **Proportions.** Are the size and shape right, especially against hands or other objects?
4. **Count and components.** Are all the parts there, and no extra ones?
5. **Materials and finish.** Does matte look matte, and glass look like glass?
6. **Shadows and reflections.** Do they behave consistently with the lighting?
7. **The honest test.** If a customer unboxed the product, would they feel misled?

If any answer is no, send it back. Good providers expect that, and revisions exist for it.

## What AI Product Photography Does Well

Used for the right jobs, AI product photography solves real problems for South African sellers:

- **Lifestyle scenes without a set.** Your product on a bathroom shelf, a picnic table or a styled desk, without hiring a location, a stylist and a photographer. Furniture and home brands feel this most; see our guide to [furniture and home décor](/blog/ai-product-images-furniture-home-decor-south-africa).
- **Variations at scale.** Every colourway, finish or size shown in the same style, built from real reference photos of each.
- **Seasonal refreshes.** Festive, gifting and sale versions of the same product, on time for [Black Friday and the festive season](/blog/black-friday-festive-season-adverts-south-africa).
- **Advert creative.** Vertical and feed formats with room for a headline, ready for Reels, TikTok and Facebook. Our [video ad sizes guide](/blog/social-media-video-ad-sizes) covers the exact dimensions.
- **Consistency across a range.** A whole catalogue that looks like one brand instead of a collection of snapshots.
- **Speed.** Days rather than weeks, which matters when stock lands before a promotion.

## Where You Still Need a Real Photo

AI is not the right tool for everything. Keep a real photograph, or a faithful edit of one, for:

- **The main marketplace image.** It's the search-result image and must show exactly what ships.
- **Anything that acts as evidence.** Certifications, ingredient panels, size markings and warning labels.
- **Colour-critical products.** Cosmetics, paint, textiles and anything where a slight shift creates returns.
- **True scale.** Shots meant to show real size need accurate proportions.
- **Regulated products.** Where claims are checked, the image must match the product and the claim.
- **Products that have never been photographed.** AI needs a real starting point; it can't invent an accurate one.

The best results come from mixing the two: real photos for the shots that must be exact, AI for everything that would otherwise need a set.

## Product Image Rules for South African Sellers

Marketplaces have their own image rules, and they're stricter than most sellers expect. Here are the main ones. Treat them as a starting point: specifications vary between sources and change over time, so always confirm current rules in each platform's seller tools.

| Platform | Main image background | Product fill | Size guidance | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Takealot** | Pure white (RGB 255, 255, 255) | 85 to 95% of frame | 1500 × 1500 px recommended | Primary image: photograph only, no text, watermarks or borders |
| **Amazon** | Pure white (RGB 255, 255, 255) | At least 85% | 1000 px minimum; 2000 × 2000 px enables zoom | Must be the actual product, no text, logos or inset images |
| **Your own store (Shopify)** | Flexible | Keep consistent | 2048 × 2048 px square | Aim for roughly 100 to 300 KB per image |
| **Instagram and Facebook** | Flexible | Not applicable | 1080 × 1350 px (4:5) or 1080 × 1080 px | 9:16 at 1080 × 1920 px for Reels and Stories |

### Takealot

Third-party summaries of Takealot's rules ([Flatlay Studio](https://flatlaystudio.co.za/pages/takealot-image-requirements), updated July 2023, and [Takealytics](https://www.takealytics.co.za/blog/how-to-create-great-product-imagery-for-takealot), July 2024) agree on the essentials: a solid white background, the product filling 85 to 95% of the frame, JPEG or PNG in sRGB, and a primary image that is a photograph with no text, watermarks or borders. Lifestyle and packaging images belong in the additional slots.

They disagree on details such as the minimum pixel size (600 px in some, 1500 × 1500 px recommended in others) and file size limits (2 MB to 10 MB). That's exactly why the Takealot Seller Portal is the authority, and why 1500 × 1500 px or larger is a safe target.

### Amazon

Amazon's main image must have a pure white background, the product filling at least 85% of the frame, and no text, logos or inset images ([Seller Labs](https://www.sellerlabs.com/blog/amazon-product-image-requirements-2026/)). Seller-policy summaries say Amazon accepts AI-enhanced images as long as they accurately represent the physical product, and that a synthetic render, illustration or mockup can't stand in for the actual product in the main slot.

### Your own store

Shopify's own guidance is a square 2048 × 2048 px image, which supports zoom without bloating page load time ([Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/blog/image-sizes)). Consistency matters more than the exact figure: use the same size and framing across every product.

### The practical rule

Keep marketplace main images real, or faithfully edited from a real photo. Use AI for secondary images, lifestyle scenes, adverts and your own store. That keeps you inside the rules, and it's where AI gives the biggest return.

## Common Product Photography Mistakes

- **Using an AI image as the main marketplace photo without checking it.** It's the image with the strictest rules and the highest cost of getting it wrong.
- **Trusting label text.** Garbled or invented lettering is the most common AI error. Read every word.
- **Trusting colour.** Screens differ, but the product must match reality in daylight.
- **Showing something you don't sell.** Extra accessories, wrong sizes or upgraded finishes create disputes.
- **Inconsistent framing.** Different sizes, angles and lighting across a catalogue make a store look untrustworthy.
- **Skipping scale.** Customers can't judge size from a product on white.
- **One image per listing.** More views, more confidence, fewer returns.
- **Forgetting formats.** A great image cropped badly for Reels or a marketplace thumbnail still loses the sale.
