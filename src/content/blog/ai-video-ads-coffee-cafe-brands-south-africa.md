Coffee content performs exceptionally well on social media — and it has for years. The pour, the steam, the latte art, the beans in close-up: there's a whole visual language around coffee that audiences engage with, save, and share. Cafés and specialty coffee brands that show up consistently with that kind of imagery build audiences faster than almost any other food and beverage category.

The problem is that producing that content at any real quality takes work. A properly lit overhead pour shot, a steam macro, a café atmosphere reel — these aren't phone-camera moments. They require lighting, equipment, time, and usually a photographer or videographer who understands food and beverage.

For an independent café or a growing specialty roaster, that production infrastructure is rarely practical to maintain week in and week out.

## What Consistent Coffee Content Actually Costs

The visual content that makes a coffee brand's social presence feel premium involves more moving parts than it looks:

- A food and beverage photographer or videographer who understands the category
- Controlled or supplemented lighting to make steam, texture and colour look right on camera
- Styling decisions — the cup, the surface, the background, the props — which all have to be right for the shot to work
- Editing time to cut, colour-grade and format for multiple platforms
- Repeat shoots as the menu changes, seasonal specials launch, or new roasts come in

Done properly, a monthly content shoot for a café or coffee brand costs R8,000–R15,000. Done inconsistently — with a mix of phone shots and occasional professional content — it's harder to build the visual identity that turns casual followers into regulars.

One well-produced shoot gives a café a burst of content. But social platforms reward consistency more than occasional excellence — an account that posts three times a week outperforms one that posts once a month, even if the once-a-month post is technically better.
