Financial services is one of the hardest categories to advertise well. The product is invisible, the stakes are high, and the rules are strict. That is exactly why short, clear video adverts can work so well here: when trust is the product, a calm explanation of what you do beats a stock photo of a handshake.

It's also why AI adverts need a careful hand in this industry. This guide covers what South African financial advisors, insurance brokers and credit providers can safely use AI adverts for, which rules shape the wording, and a pre-flight checklist you can run before an advert goes to your compliance officer.

> **Key takeaways**
>
> - **The rules don't change because an advert is AI-made.** The FAIS General Code of Conduct and, for credit, the National Credit Act apply in the same way.
> - **AI is a good fit for explaining.** Short explainers on what cover means, how a budget works or what an advisor does build trust without making promises.
> - **AI is a poor fit for proof.** No synthetic customers, claim stories, awards or returns.
> - **Disclosures take screen time.** Plan for them at the start, because a 15-second advert has little room.
> - **This is guidance, not legal advice.** Get final adverts approved by your key individual or compliance officer.

## The Rules That Shape Financial Adverts

Two main frameworks apply, and your product decides which one you're under. The summaries below come from published compliance guidance rather than the gazetted text, so confirm the current wording with your compliance officer or the [Financial Sector Conduct Authority (FSCA)](https://www.fsca.co.za/).

**FAIS (advisors, brokers and insurers).**

- **Licence reference.** Section 8(b) of the FAIS Act requires a [reference to the fact that your licence is held](https://www.horizoncompliance.co.za/complianceblog/fais-and-adverts) in advertisements and other promotional material.
- **No untrue or misleading statements.** Section 14 of the General Code of Conduct says an advert must not contain a statement, promise or forecast that is fraudulent, untrue or misleading.
- **Balance and plain language.** Adverts should be [factually correct, clear, balanced and in plain language](https://www.moonstone.co.za/amending-the-rules-of-advertising-how-to-keep-your-ads-safe-and-within-legal-limits/), and shouldn't belittle other providers or products.
- **Performance claims.** Performance data, including awards and rankings, should state its source and date, and past performance should carry a warning that it isn't necessarily indicative of the future.
- **Risk and guarantee warnings.** Where it applies, a risk warning should be displayed prominently, with a warning that there are no guarantees if the value isn't guaranteed.
- **Genuine testimonials.** Endorsements must reflect real opinion and actual experience.
- **Approval and records.** Adverts should be approved by the key individual or a delegate through a documented process, and records of adverts are kept.

**National Credit Act (loans and credit).**

- Credit providers must be registered with the [National Credit Regulator](https://www.ncr.org.za/).
- If an advert only mentions that credit is available, extra cost disclosure isn't required. If it mentions costs, interest rates or monthly repayments, the required information must be included, displayed together and in text [no smaller than the average font size in the advert](https://pbsa.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/NATIONAL-CREDIT-ACT-REGULATIONS.pdf).

More general rules apply as well, including the [Advertising Regulatory Board's Code](https://www.arb.org.za/), which our [complete guide to AI advertising](/blog/ai-advertising-south-africa-guide) covers.

## What AI Adverts Do Well in Financial Services

Think of an AI advert in this industry as a patient explainer, not a sales pitch.

- **Explainers.** "What does income protection actually cover?" "How does a monthly budget work?" A short animated advert that defines one idea in plain language builds credibility and reduces anxiety about the subject.
- **Who you are and how you work.** A brand film that introduces your practice, your process and how a first meeting works.
- **Practical reminders.** Policy review reminders, new office hours, a new advisor joining, how to submit a claim.
- **Education series.** Weekly short adverts on money basics, which suits a monthly package. See our guide to [AI social media management](/blog/ai-social-media-management-small-business-south-africa).
- **Recruitment.** Hiring advisors, administrators or call-centre staff.
- **WhatsApp Status.** A clear, one-idea advert with your contact details. See our [WhatsApp Status guide](/blog/whatsapp-status-video-ads-south-africa).

These formats don't need a film crew, an office shoot or a presenter who has to be scheduled around client meetings, which is often what holds financial practices back.

## What to Keep AI Away From

- **Synthetic customers and testimonials.** An AI-generated "happy client" saying how much they saved is a fabricated testimonial, whatever disclaimer sits underneath it.
- **Invented claim stories.** A dramatised claim is fine if it's clearly an illustration. A "real" claim story that never happened is not.
- **Awards, rankings and ratings you didn't earn.** If it's real, state the source and date. If it isn't, leave it out.
- **Projected returns shown as certainties.** Illustrations need stated assumptions, and a statement that they aren't guaranteed.
- **AI "advisors" who look like real people.** If a person on screen represents your practice, they should be a real member of your team, or clearly an illustrated character.
- **Regulator branding.** Don't use the FSCA's logo or name in a way that implies endorsement.
- **Pressure and fear.** "Don't be left with nothing" and countdown timers sit badly with a requirement to be balanced and not misleading.

## How to Brief a Financial Services Advert

A good brief makes compliance easier, because the right decisions are made before the first draft. Our guide on [how to brief an AI advert](/blog/how-to-brief-an-ai-advert-south-africa) covers the basics. For financial services, add:

1. **The one idea.** One concept, such as "what is income protection?", not three.
2. **The required wording.** Send us the licence reference and any disclosures your compliance officer requires, so they are designed into the advert instead of squeezed in at the end.
3. **The format and length.** Longer disclosures need longer or static formats. See our [video ad sizes guide](/blog/social-media-video-ad-sizes) for safe zones, so text isn't hidden behind platform buttons.
4. **Who approves.** Tell us who signs off, and allow time for it, since approval is part of the schedule.
5. **What not to include.** Awards, figures and testimonials only if they are real and documented.
