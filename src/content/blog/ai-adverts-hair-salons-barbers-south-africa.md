A haircut, a fade or a set of gel nails is sold by how it looks, and a salon's marketing lives on Instagram, Facebook and WhatsApp Status. The work itself is often brilliant. The marketing around it is usually a patchy feed, a photo of a handwritten price list and a WhatsApp group that goes quiet whenever the diary is busy.

Hair salons, barbershops and nail studios also have a problem most marketing advice ignores: the best proof of your work has a real person's face attached to it. Getting that right matters legally and personally, and it changes how AI adverts should be used.

## Why Salon and Barber Marketing Is Hard

- **Quiet days cost the same as busy ones.** Saturday books itself. Tuesday doesn't, but rent, chair hire and stock still have to be paid.
- **Peak seasons arrive in waves.** Matric dance season, weddings and the festive period all need their own adverts, ready before clients book elsewhere.
- **Your best work has a face on it.** Results are photographed on real clients, which raises consent and privacy questions.
- **Content comes in bursts.** A busy stylist posts between appointments, then not at all for two weeks.
- **Price lists keep changing.** A menu photographed in March is out of date by June.

## Client Photos and POPIA

When a business posts a photograph of an identifiable client to promote itself, it's processing personal information. Law firm Werksmans, writing on [POPIA and social media photographs](https://www.polity.org.za/article/popia-and-social-media-posts-2021-06-15), notes that POPIA's conditions include obtaining the person's consent before posting their photograph, and that the personal-use exemption doesn't stretch to business promotion.

In practice, that means:

- **Ask before you post.** A short written release, or a saved WhatsApp message agreeing to it, is far better than assuming.
- **Say where it will be used.** Instagram, your website and paid adverts are different uses.
- **Let clients withdraw.** If someone asks you to take a photo down, do it.
- **Be careful with minors.** A parent's consent is needed for a child's photo.

This is general information, not legal advice. If you're unsure, ask a professional.

### Where AI Helps, and Where It Shouldn't

AI lets you build promotional content that doesn't need a client in it at all: menus, offers, seasonal campaigns and atmosphere. That removes a lot of the consent paperwork from everyday marketing.

What it must not do is fake your results. An AI-generated "after" that suggests a finish, colour or cut you can't deliver is a misleading representation. Section 41 of the Consumer Protection Act prohibits false, misleading or deceptive representations, and the Advertising Regulatory Board's Code requires advertising to be truthful. Our [complete guide to AI advertising](/blog/ai-advertising-south-africa-guide) covers those rules in more detail.

The sensible split is simple: **AI for atmosphere, menus and promotions; real photos, with consent, for results.**

## What to Keep Real

The other half of the split matters just as much. Keep real photographs, and real people, for:

- **Results on real clients,** with their consent, since these are your strongest proof.
- **Your stylists and barbers.** Clients book people, not just services.
- **Your actual salon.** AI can polish the lighting, but it shouldn't show a space you don't have.
- **Reviews and testimonials.** Real ones only, and never invented.
- **Any style you don't offer.** If you can't do it, don't advertise it.
